Idea

Where the name comes from

In film, a project is greenlit when it is approved to go ahead. In an audit, the reviewer puts a tick, traditionally in green, against every line they have checked. Greenlit uses both meanings: release work is checked line by line, and the release goes ahead once the ticks are in.

MissionMake every release decision traceable to evidence.
PromiseIf it was found, it is filed. If it was fixed, it was retested. If it shipped, there is proof.
Who it is forQA engineers, QA leads and agencies who test several products at once and have to answer for what went out.
What it isThe record that links requirements, tests, evidence and defects, and shows what is still open before a release goes out.

Three working principles

1

Connect, do not replace

Teams keep testing in their own tools and keep bugs in Jira. Greenlit records what happened and checks that nothing was skipped.

2

AI drafts, people approve

Generated plans, cases and reports stay marked as drafts until a named person signs them off.

3

Say what is still open

Open items come first, with their age, their owner and what is missing.

Greenlit is

A record of what was tested, what was found and what was fixed.
The link between requirements, tests, evidence and defects.
The place where AI drafts are reviewed and signed off.
A report you can hand to a client.

Greenlit is not

A test runner or automation tool.
An issue tracker, or a Jira replacement.
A place for sprint planning or developer tasks.
A spreadsheet with nicer colours.

Voice

How Greenlit writes

People open Greenlit when a release is close and something may be wrong. The writing has to be quick to read and precise enough to act on.

ExactGive the ID, the number and the date: “BUG-1042, fixed 4 days ago”, not “an older issue”.
LevelReport risk without drama. No exclamation marks and no capital-letter warnings.
AnswerableEvery figure can be traced to the evidence behind it.
On the tester’s sidePoint at the gap, not the person. Say what is missing, not who missed it.
Write thisNot this
 The release is not ready. 3 fixes have not been retested. WARNING: your release is at risk!!!
 BUG-1042 is marked Done in Jira but has no retest evidence. Looks like someone forgot to test this one.
 Draft test plan: 42 cases from your PRD. Review before approving. Your AI test plan is ready. You’re all set!
 Button: “Sign off release 2.4.1” Button: “Submit”

House rules. Sentence case for headings, buttons and labels. British spelling. Buttons start with a verb. Numbers before adjectives. AI output is a draft until someone approves it. No exclamation marks and no emoji in the product.

Tone by moment

MomentExample
A release is blocked3 fixes need a retest before this release can be signed off.
An AI draft is readyDraft ready. Review the 42 cases before approving.
Something went wrongThe Jira sync failed at 14:32. Reconnect Jira to try again.

Logo

The logo

A serif wordmark with a hand-drawn tick after it, the way a reviewer marks a line in the margin. The tick is the only green in the logo.

Reversed, on ink. The tick lifts to a lighter green.

On green. The tick turns to paper.

The tick alone, for icons and favicons.

How the tick is drawn

1A short stroke with a round start, rising at about 45°.
2A longer stroke that curves slightly and thins to a point, like a pen lifting off.
3The wordmark is Newsreader Semibold at its largest optical size, tracked tight (−2.8%) and converted to outlines.

Always use the supplied SVG or PNG files. The tick is not a font character and cannot be retyped.

Marks and status

Marks and status

Status is shown the way a reviewer would mark a page: a tick, a cross, a dash, an empty box. Colour backs the mark up. It never carries the meaning alone.

MarkStatusMeaningIn the product
PassedThe test ran and met its expected result.Passed
FailedThe test ran and did not. Also used for a failed retest.Retest failed
On holdBlocked, or fixed and waiting for a retest.Needs retest
Not runAn empty box: not yet run.Not run
StaleA dashed box: not run in 30 days, or its requirement changed.Stale · 42d
DraftBlue pencil: anything a person has not yet approved, including AI output.Draft
VerifiedA circled tick: fixed, retested and backed by evidence. The highest state.Verified

Rules

Always pair a mark, a label and a colour. The mark alone must still read in black and white.
Green means verified or ready to go. Nothing else in the product is green.
Red means failed. It is never used for emphasis, branding or “important”.

Sizes

Marks are drawn on a 16 px grid with a 1.7 px stroke, round ends and round joins. They scale with the text beside them and are never used below 12 px.

12, 16, 24 and 32 px.

Layout

Layout: the margin

Greenlit documents use a ledger layout. A narrow margin on the left holds numbers and ticks, a hairline separates it from the content, and everything else sits to the right. This guide, reports and exports all use it.

Vocabulary

These are product terms. Use them exactly, in the app, the docs, sales decks and support replies.

TermMeansNot for
VerifiedFixed and retested by QA, with evidence attached.A ticket closed in Jira
ClosedThe issue tracker’s status. Greenlit reports it and does not own it.“Done” or “fixed”
Needs retestMarked fixed by engineering, not yet retested by QA.New or open defects
StaleA test not run in 30 days, or whose linked requirement has changed.Failed tests
QA debtA weighted score of open QA work: unretested fixes, ageing defects, stale tests, uncovered requirements.A bug count
ReadinessGreenlit’s assessment before a release: Ready, or Not ready yet.“Approved”
Sign-offA named person’s approval, recorded with a reason and a date.AI output
DraftAny AI-generated plan, case or report before someone approves it.Approved content
